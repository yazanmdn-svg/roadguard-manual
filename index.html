01

Project Functions & Purpose

🚛

RoadGuard is an autonomous IoT-based truck designed to solve two of Jordan's most critical road infrastructure problems: potholes and underground water pipe leaks. The system operates without continuous human intervention — detecting faults, generating repair reports, requesting institutional approval, and executing repairs automatically.

ℹ️
Who is this system for?

Municipalities, the Ministry of Public Works, and road maintenance authorities in Jordan. Operators manage the system through the RoadGuard dashboard without needing to be physically present with the truck.

🔬
Pothole Detection
Uses ultrasonic sensors and a mounted camera to scan road surfaces in real time, identifying potholes, cracks, and surface deformations with ≥90% accuracy.
💧
Water Leak Detection
Moisture and temperature sensors detect abnormal surface humidity patterns that indicate underground pipe leaks before they cause major road damage.
📋
Auto Report Generation
Every detected fault is automatically documented with GPS coordinates, severity rating, required materials, and cost estimate — sent instantly to the dashboard.
✅
Approval Workflow
Institutions review and approve repair reports on the dashboard. Once approved, the truck navigates autonomously to the location and performs the repair.
🛠️
Autonomous Repair
The truck dispenses cold-mix asphalt into potholes with precision servo control, performing quick repairs that prevent recurring damage.
📊
Live Monitoring Dashboard
A cloud-connected dashboard shows real-time truck location, fault map, repair status, battery level, and sensor health — accessible from any device.

How to Operate the Truck

🕹️

The truck operates in two modes: Autonomous Mode (recommended) and Manual Override Mode. This section covers both. Always follow the pre-operation checklist before deployment.

⚠️
Before you begin

Always complete the Pre-Operation Checklist before deploying the truck on any road. Skipping this may result in system errors or unsafe operation.

✅ Pre-Operation Checklist

  • ✓
    Battery level is above 30% (check LED indicator or dashboard)
  • ✓
    Asphalt reservoir is filled (minimum 50% for deployment)
  • ✓
    All sensors show green status in the dashboard
  • ✓
    GPS signal is acquired (shown by solid blue LED)
  • ✓
    4G/Wi-Fi connection is active and dashboard is reachable
  • ✓
    Camera lens is clean and unobstructed
  • ✓
    Emergency stop button has been tested
  • ✓
    Target patrol route is defined in the dashboard

🤖 Autonomous Mode (Recommended)

1

Power On the System

Press and hold the green power button on the truck's control panel for 3 seconds. Wait for the startup sequence: all LEDs will flash once, then the status LED will turn solid green, indicating the system is ready.

⏱ Startup takes approximately 45 seconds
2

Open the Dashboard

On any browser, navigate to your institution's RoadGuard dashboard URL. Log in with your authorized credentials. Confirm the truck appears as "Online" in the Fleet panel.

3

Define the Patrol Route

On the Live Map, click "New Route" and draw the road segment you want the truck to patrol. You can also upload a predefined route file (.JSON format). Set the patrol speed (default: 5 km/h).

4

Start Autonomous Patrol

Click the "Start Patrol" button in the dashboard. The truck will autonomously navigate the route, scanning every meter of road surface. Detected faults appear instantly on the live map as color-coded markers.

🔴 Red = Pothole    🔵 Blue = Water Leak    🟢 Green = Repaired
5

Review & Approve Repair Reports

When a fault is detected, a repair report is automatically generated and appears in the "Pending Approval" queue. Review the details and click "Approve" to authorize the truck to perform the repair.

6

Monitor Repair Execution

After approval, the truck navigates to the fault location and performs the repair. Monitor progress in real time on the dashboard. A completion notification is sent when the repair is verified.

🕹️ Manual Override Mode

Manual override is available for testing, repositioning, or emergency situations. Use the handheld remote controller included in the kit, or switch to Manual Mode in the dashboard.

03

Installation & Setup

⚙️

This section covers the complete installation process — from unboxing the hardware to connecting the system and uploading the firmware for the first time.

✅
What's in the box

RoadGuard Truck Unit, handheld remote controller, USB-C charging cable, 4G SIM card (pre-configured), cold-mix asphalt starter pack (2 kg), quick-start card, and this user manual.

🔧 Hardware Setup

1

Unbox & Inspect

Remove the truck carefully from its packaging. Inspect all sensors, the camera module, and the chassis for any shipping damage. Ensure the ultrasonic sensors (front, left, right) are properly mounted and unobstructed.

2

Insert the SIM Card

Open the communication module panel on the rear-left side of the truck. Insert the pre-configured 4G SIM card into the slot until it clicks. Close the panel securely to maintain IP65 water resistance.

📌 The SIM supports Zain JO, Orange JO, and Umniah networks
3

Charge the Battery

Connect the USB-C cable to the charging port (bottom-right panel). Charge until the battery LED turns solid green — approximately 3–4 hours for a full charge. Do not operate with battery below 15%.

4

Fill the Asphalt Reservoir

Open the top access panel and locate the asphalt reservoir. Pour cold-mix asphalt material into the reservoir until it reaches the MAX line indicator. Close and seal the panel. Do not overfill.

5

Upload Firmware (First Time Only)

Connect the truck to your computer via USB-C. Open the RoadGuard Setup Tool (downloadable from the dashboard). Click "Flash Firmware" and wait for the process to complete. The truck will restart automatically.

🌐 Software & Dashboard Setup

1

Create Your Institution Account

Contact your system administrator or email roadguard@ju.edu.jo to create your institution account. You will receive login credentials by email within 24 hours.

2

Register Your Truck

Log into the dashboard, go to Settings → Fleet Management → Add Truck. Enter the Truck ID printed on the bottom of the unit (format: RG-XXXX). The truck will appear in your fleet within 2 minutes of powering on.

3

Configure Alert Preferences

Go to Settings → Notifications. Set up SMS or email alerts for critical fault detections, low battery warnings, and repair completion notifications. Add all relevant staff members.

4

Run the Calibration Routine

Place the truck on a flat, obstruction-free surface. In the dashboard, go to Fleet → [Your Truck] → Calibrate. Follow the on-screen instructions. This calibrates all sensors for accurate local readings. Repeat this every 30 days.

⚠️
Important: First Deployment Safety Check

Before the first outdoor deployment, run the truck in a controlled area (e.g., a parking lot) for at least 10 minutes in Manual Mode to verify all motors, sensors, and communication channels are functioning correctly.

Connecting to the IoT Server & Dashboard

📡

The RoadGuard IoT system uses a cloud-based architecture. The truck communicates with the MQTT broker over 4G, which then syncs with the web dashboard. This section explains how to connect, configure, and interpret the live data stream.

🔐
Access the Dashboard
Open your browser and navigate to your institution's RoadGuard URL. Login credentials are provided by your system administrator. Use Chrome or Firefox for best compatibility.
📶
Connection Requirements
The truck requires 4G signal to communicate. The dashboard operator needs only a standard internet connection. Minimum: 1 Mbps upload for real-time map updates.

🔌 MQTT Connection Settings

// RoadGuard MQTT Broker Configuration

  "topics": {

    "location": "roadguard/truck/gps",
    "status": "roadguard/truck/status",
    "repairs": "roadguard/repairs/complete"

  "keep_alive": 60,  // seconds

}

📥 Receiving & Approving Repair Reports

1

Receive Notification

When the truck detects a fault, you receive an instant dashboard notification and optionally an SMS/email alert (if configured). The report appears in the "Pending Approval" queue.

2

Review the Report

Click the report to view: fault location on map, fault type (pothole/water leak), measured depth, recommended materials, estimated cost in JOD, and severity classification (Low/Medium/High/Critical).

3

Make a Decision

Choose one of: Approve (truck proceeds to repair immediately), Schedule (set a date/time for repair), or Reject with reason (logged for audit). Your decision is time-stamped.

4

Track Completion

After approval, track repair progress in real time. When complete, the truck re-scans the location. A "Repair Verified" status is assigned and the map marker turns green. You receive a final completion report.

📈 Running a System Diagnostic

1

Open System Diagnostics

In the dashboard, go to Fleet → [Your Truck] → Diagnostics → Run Full Check.

2

Review Results

Each subsystem is tested and reported as PASS / WARN / FAIL. All items should show PASS for safe deployment. WARN items should be addressed within 48 hours. FAIL items must be resolved before operation.

3

Export Diagnostic Report

Click "Download Report" to save a PDF of the diagnostic results. Keep this on file for maintenance records and institutional compliance.

✅
Best Practice: Weekly Verification

Run a full diagnostic check every 7 days even if no issues are reported. Clean all sensor surfaces monthly. Recalibrate sensors every 30 days for optimal accuracy.

Troubleshooting Guide

🔧

Use this guide to diagnose and resolve the most common issues encountered during operation. If the problem persists after following these steps, contact technical support.

🔴

Truck is not appearing in the dashboard

Possible causes: SIM card not properly inserted, 4G signal too weak, or truck not powered on correctly.

  1. Confirm the truck is powered on — status LED should be solid green.
  2. Check the SIM card is properly inserted and the panel is sealed.
  3. Verify 4G signal — blinking red LED indicates no connection. Move to an open area.
  4. In the dashboard, go to Fleet → Refresh. Wait 2 minutes after power-on for first connection.
  5. If still offline, go to Settings → Truck ID → Re-register and enter the truck ID again.
  6. If the problem continues, restart the truck by holding the power button for 8 seconds (hard reset).
🔴

Sensors showing incorrect or no readings

Possible causes: Sensor lens is dirty, calibration has drifted, or physical damage to the sensor.

  1. Clean all sensor surfaces with a dry microfiber cloth — especially the ultrasonic sensors and camera lens.
  2. Check for physical obstructions in front of the sensors (mud, debris).
  3. Run sensor recalibration: Dashboard → Fleet → Calibrate → Sensors Only.
  4. Inspect sensor mounting screws — loose mounting causes vibration errors.
  5. Check the diagnostic report — if a sensor is marked FAIL, it may need replacement.
🔴

Repair material not dispensing

Possible causes: Reservoir is empty, nozzle is clogged, or servo motor is not responding.

  1. Check the asphalt reservoir level — open the top panel and visually confirm material is present.
  2. Inspect the dispensing nozzle for dried asphalt blockage. Use warm water to clear if needed.
  3. In the dashboard, go to Truck → Manual Controls → Test Dispense. Listen for the servo motor sound.
  4. If the servo is silent, check the motor connection inside the control panel (requires technical access).
  5. Restart the truck and retry. If the issue persists, contact technical support.
🔴

GPS location is inaccurate or not updating

Possible causes: GPS module still acquiring signal, interference from nearby structures, or firmware issue.

  1. Move the truck to an open outdoor area away from tall buildings — GPS needs clear sky view.
  2. Wait 60–90 seconds after powering on for the GPS to acquire a stable signal (blue LED solid).
  3. Check that the GPS antenna (small black wire on top of the truck) is not bent or disconnected.
  4. In the dashboard, check the "Satellites Connected" counter — minimum 4 satellites needed for accurate location.
  5. If still inaccurate after 5 minutes, perform a GPS reset: Diagnostics → GPS → Reset Module.
🔴

Truck is not following the defined route

Possible causes: Route file error, GPS inaccuracy, or obstacle in path causing re-routing.

  1. Confirm the route was saved correctly in the dashboard before starting the patrol.
  2. Check for obstacles on the planned route that caused the truck to stop or divert.
  3. Verify GPS accuracy is ±2m or better before starting (check Fleet panel).
  4. Delete and redraw the route — avoid routes with very sharp turns (minimum turning radius is 0.5m).
  5. Switch to Manual Override to reposition, then resume autonomous mode.

Frequently Asked Questions

💬

Answers to the most commonly asked questions from operators and institutional users.

How accurate is the pothole detection system? ▼

The system achieves a target detection accuracy of ≥90% under normal conditions. Accuracy is measured as the number of correct detections divided by the total number of tests. Sensor fusion (camera + ultrasonic) significantly reduces false positives. Accuracy may decrease slightly in heavy rain or very poor lighting — the system will flag these conditions on the dashboard.

Can the truck operate in rain or extreme weather? ▼

Yes. The truck has an IP65 rating, meaning it is protected against dust and water jets from any direction. It can operate in light to moderate rain. However, operation is not recommended in heavy storms, flash flooding, or wind speeds above 50 km/h. The dashboard will display a weather caution alert when conditions are borderline.

What happens if the truck loses internet connection during a patrol? ▼

The truck continues its patrol and stores all detected fault data locally on the onboard microSD card. Once connectivity is restored, all data is automatically uploaded to the dashboard. If connection is lost for more than 10 minutes, the truck will safely return to its last known position and stop — this is a built-in safety feature.

How long does it take to repair one pothole? ▼

On average, the truck takes 3–8 minutes per pothole repair, depending on the size and depth. This includes navigation to the location, surface preparation, material dispensing, and the post-repair scan. For context, traditional manual repair with a crew takes 20–45 minutes per pothole with setup time included.

Who has the authority to approve repair reports? ▼

Only users with "Approver" or "Administrator" roles assigned by your institution can approve repair reports. Regular operators with "Viewer" access can monitor the dashboard but cannot authorize repairs. Roles are managed in Settings → User Management. This ensures compliance with institutional authorization procedures.

How often does the asphalt reservoir need to be refilled? ▼

The reservoir holds approximately 5 kg of cold-mix asphalt, which is enough for 10–20 small potholes depending on their size. The dashboard shows the material level in real time. The truck will automatically pause patrol and alert the operator when material drops below 20%. Refilling takes approximately 5 minutes.

Can multiple trucks be managed from one dashboard? ▼

Yes. The dashboard supports unlimited fleet management. Each truck appears as a separate entity on the map and in the Fleet panel. You can assign different trucks to different patrol zones simultaneously, and all repair reports are centralized in one queue. Fleet plans are available for institutions managing 5 or more trucks.

Is the data collected by the truck stored securely? ▼

Yes. All data — including GPS coordinates, sensor readings, images, and repair records — is encrypted using TLS 1.3 during transmission and AES-256 at rest in the cloud. The system complies with ISO/IEC 27001 information security standards. Data is retained for 5 years for audit purposes and can only be accessed by authorized institutional users.

What is the cost of repairing a pothole using this system? ▼

The estimated cost per pothole repair using the RoadGuard system is 11–20 JOD, including distributed equipment cost, materials (cold-mix asphalt), labor, and fuel. This compares favorably to traditional repair methods which can cost 20–30 JOD per pothole when factoring in crew, equipment, and logistics costs.

Policies & Guidelines

📋

All operators and institutional users of the RoadGuard system are required to follow these policies. These guidelines ensure safe, legal, and ethical operation in compliance with Jordanian law and international engineering standards.

⚠️ Safety Policy

  • Public Road Deployment: The truck must only operate with appropriate road safety measures in place, including warning signs or cones placed at least 10 meters ahead of the truck's operating area.
  • Speed Limit: Maximum autonomous operation speed is 8 km/h on public roads. Never override this limit.
  • Supervision: A trained operator must be reachable via the dashboard at all times during autonomous operation — the system must not be left entirely unattended.
  • Emergency Stop: All operators must know how to trigger the Emergency Stop from both the dashboard and the handheld remote before any deployment.
  • Weather Conditions: Do not operate during heavy rain, storms, or visibility below 50 meters.
  • Night Operation: Night operation is only permitted with additional lighting attachments installed on the truck.

🔒 Data & Privacy Policy

  • Data Ownership: All road condition data, images, and repair records collected by the system are the property of the operating institution.
  • Data Sharing: Data must not be shared with third parties without written authorization from the institution's designated data officer.
  • Image Capture: The truck's camera is configured to capture road surfaces only — not pedestrians or vehicles. Do not modify the camera angle to capture individuals.
  • Compliance: All data handling complies with ISO/IEC 27001 and Jordan's Telecommunications Law No. 13 of 1995.
  • Access Control: Login credentials must not be shared. Each user must have their own account. Report compromised accounts immediately.

🏛️ Institutional Authorization Policy

  • Approval Authority: Only designated Approvers may authorize repair operations. No repairs should be executed without institutional approval logged in the system.
  • Audit Trail: Every decision (approve, reject, schedule) is recorded with the approver's name, timestamp, and reason. This record is immutable and cannot be deleted.
  • Budget Compliance: Repair approvals must stay within the institution's allocated maintenance budget. The dashboard provides real-time spending tracking.
  • Coordination: Before deploying on any road segment, coordinate with the relevant municipality or authority to ensure no conflicting maintenance work is scheduled.

🔧 Maintenance & Equipment Policy

  • Routine Maintenance: A formal maintenance check must be performed every 30 days, including sensor calibration, battery health check, and mechanical inspection.
  • Repair Material: Only approved cold-mix asphalt materials that comply with Jordanian road standards should be used. Do not substitute with unapproved materials.
  • Firmware Updates: Apply all system firmware updates within 14 days of release. Outdated firmware may cause security vulnerabilities or performance degradation.
  • Damage Reporting: Any physical damage to the truck must be reported to the system administrator within 24 hours and logged in the maintenance record.

⚖️ Legal & Regulatory Compliance

  • Jordan Traffic Law No. 49 (2008): The truck must comply with all rules governing special-purpose vehicles on public roads, including the use of hazard lights during operation.
  • Telecommunications Law No. 13 (1995): The 4G communication module must use a properly licensed SIM card from an authorized Jordanian telecommunications provider.
  • IEEE Code of Ethics: All engineers operating or developing the system must uphold the IEEE Code of Ethics, particularly regarding public safety (Code 1) and honest reporting (Code 3).
  • ISO 13473 Compliance: Road condition measurements and assessments must follow the pavement characterization standards defined in ISO 13473.
  • ISO/IEC 27001: Information security practices must adhere to ISO/IEC 27001 standards for all data collected, transmitted, and stored by the system.
🚫
Strictly Prohibited

Modifying the truck's hardware or firmware without authorization · Operating on private property without owner consent · Using the system for surveillance or data collection beyond road conditions · Sharing institutional login credentials · Attempting to bypass the approval workflow for repairs.
